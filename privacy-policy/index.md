# Fact Tree privacy policy

_Effective: 5 November 2026._

Fact Tree is an iPhone app of short, sourced facts, made by Jessey Zheng ("we"). This policy says what the app does with information about you. In short: **the app collects nothing about you.** There is no account, no
analytics, no advertising and no tracking, and what you read stays on your iPhone.

## No account, no sign-in
You never sign in or create an account. The app doesn't ask for your name, email address, phone
number or any other personal detail.

## What stays on your iPhone
The app keeps its facts, and a note of which facts you've already seen and when you first saw
each one, in a database on your iPhone. That note powers "unseen first" in the reels and the
Stats tab. It never leaves your iPhone through the app: it isn't uploaded, synced to iCloud by the
app, or shared with anyone. Like other app data, it's part of your iPhone's own backups if you use
them. Deleting the app deletes it.

The app saves no settings or preferences. Its diagnostic messages, which record only errors,
counts, fact IDs and corpus and feed versions, go to your iPhone's system log and stay on your iPhone.

## No analytics, advertising or tracking
The app contains no analytics, advertising, crash-reporting or other third-party code. It doesn't
track you across apps or websites, and it doesn't use your location, contacts, photos, camera or
microphone.

## When the app goes online
The app works fully offline. It connects to the internet in only two cases.

**New facts.** Once new facts are published, the app checks for them once each time it starts, by
downloading one public file. (Until then it makes no connection for this.) The request carries no
account, no identifier, no cookies and nothing about what you've read, and the app keeps no copy of
it. Like any web request, it reaches the server with your IP address and the standard headers
iPhone apps send, such as the app's build number and language settings. The web host may keep
these in its normal server logs under its own policy. We don't use them to identify or follow you.

**Source and licence pages.** Every fact has a link to its source, and Settings links to each
source's licence. Opening one loads that page from the publisher's website. The publisher's own
privacy practices, such as cookies and logs, apply to its pages, whether they open in your browser
or inside the app.

## Reports and messages are emails you send
"Report a problem" and "Contact" open your own email app with a message addressed to us. It may
already hold the app's version number and, when you report from a fact, that fact. Nothing is sent unless you send it. If you do, we receive what
you wrote and your email address, and we use them only to answer you and correct the fact. We don't
add you to any list or share your email with anyone.

We keep your messages only as long as we need them to answer you and fix the fact, and then delete
them. You can ask us at any time to delete your messages, and we will.

## Crash reports come from Apple
If you've agreed to share analytics with app developers in your iPhone's settings, or if you're
testing the app through TestFlight, Apple may send us crash reports. Apple collects these, not the
app, under Apple's privacy policy. They tell us what went wrong in the app, and we use them only to
fix bugs. For the App Store app, you can stop sharing them at any time in Settings › Privacy &
Security › Analytics & Improvements, by turning off Share With App Developers.

## Your data and deleting it
We share no data with anyone, because the app collects none. What the app keeps on your iPhone is
removed when you delete the app. Emails you send us are kept and deleted as described above.

## Children
The app collects no personal information from anyone, including children. If a child emails us,
we treat the message as described under "Reports and messages are emails you send".

## Changes
If the app ever starts handling your information differently, we'll update this policy and its
date before that version of the app is released.
